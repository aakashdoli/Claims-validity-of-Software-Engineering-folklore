# Claims Validity of Software Engineering Folklore

Master's thesis · PA2534 VT26 · Blekinge Institute of Technology

**Authors** Aakash Doli · Ekshith Satnur
**Supervisors** Davide Fucci (BTH) · Greg Wilson (Third Bit)

---

Practitioner software engineering books are full of confident advice. This
thesis asks which of those claims hold up: what the books assert, what the
empirical literature says, and what practitioners actually believe.

**5 of 28 scored claims (17.9%) are belief–evidence mismatches — 4 believed
despite contradicting evidence, 1 disbelieved despite supporting evidence.**

---

## Tools

| Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|
| [rq1-extraction-pipeline/](rq1-extraction-pipeline/) | Extracts claims from practitioner books and classifies them by claim type, using a rule-based pre-filter followed by a language model classifier. |
| [rq3-survey-analysis-tool/](rq3-survey-analysis-tool/) | Analyses practitioner survey responses and cross-tabulates what people believe against what the evidence review found. |

Setup and usage instructions live in each tool's own README.

Verified pipeline output for the run cited throughout the thesis is in
`rq3-survey-analysis-tool/data/results/20260904T170248Z-d602c796/`, including
the belief-evidence matrix, evidence label distribution, and subgroup
comparison outputs.
